Three Sales Agents, One Client

Inside a real estate CRM where every agent plays to win, and only the admin sees the whole game.

Omar Einshouka · 11 October 2026

1. What is a CRM, and why does real estate need one?

A CRM (customer relationship management system) is the one place where a company keeps everything about its clients: who they are, what they’re looking for, where they came from, and every call, message and meeting since the first contact. In most real estate offices I’ve seen, that information is spread across WhatsApp chats and spreadsheets, and leads get lost somewhere in between.

A good CRM makes the daily work lighter for everyone. Marketing can see that its leads actually reached sales. Sales agents know who to call next and don’t forget what they promised. Managers can see the whole pipeline without chasing people for updates, and they can tell which sources and which people are bringing in contracts.

2. Where most CRMs fall short

In real estate, a good lead rarely belongs to one sales agent. One makes the first call, another sends the payment plan, a third books the site visit. Many of the CRMs we tried have a single status per lead, so every update erases the one before it, and the manager is left guessing who did what.

Over the past few months I worked on a CRM for a real estate startup that wants to grow in a crowded market. We kept coming back to one idea: let many sales agents work the same lead, each with their own actions and their own feedback, and let the admin follow the progress of each sales agent on every lead.

3. What makes it different

Our signature is that several sales agents can work the same lead while the admin watches the competition how each of them is doing on it and control every thing smoothly. Around that, every role gets what it needs and nothing more.

  • Marketing always knows where its leads stand, and gets a notification when one is assigned or moves.
  • Every sales agent has a diary and reminders, so promises don’t slip.
  • The admin sees and traces everything: each agent’s progress on every lead, everyone’s performance, and the full history of every lead.
  • It works on the phone. One tap to call or WhatsApp a client.
  • It’s built so it can grow into a SaaS, with many companies on one platform.

4. How a lead moves

A lead comes in from a campaign, an import or a walk-in. The CRM checks the phone number first, so the same client doesn’t get called twice by mistake. A manager then hands the lead to one sales agent or several. They call, message and log what happened, and each log moves the lead along an eleven-step ladder, from New to Contract signed.

5. The pipeline feedback

Every buyer sounds different on the first call, but when we studied how clients behave, the same moments kept coming back. Some don’t pick up, some ask for a call back, some want a site visit, and some sign. So we turned every one of those moments into a progress pipeline. After any call, message or note, the sales agent just picks where the client stands now.

7. The sales journey

A sales agent works their own leads, logs what happens, and lets the diary remember the rest. They can’t see which marketing agent brought a lead in, or that a colleague might be working the same client. As far as each agent is concerned, the lead is theirs to win. That keeps the team competitive, and it keeps client details on a need-to-know basis.

8. The admin journey: our signature

This is the part of the system I care about most.

In real estate it’s normal for a hot lead to pass through more than one agent, and most tools make you pick a single owner. Here the admin can put several sales agents on the same lead on purpose. Each one works it in their own way and logs their own feedback, and none of them can see the others. The admin is the only one who sees the whole picture: who is still trying to reach the client, who is following up, and who has already taken them to see the site.

It’s a quiet competition between agents, and the admin has the only seat with a full view.

9. On the phone

Every screen works on a phone. Tap a number and it asks “Call or WhatsApp?”, and the agent can log what happened before they’ve even put the phone down.

10. The architecture of the CRM system

Under the hood it’s one program on one server, talking to one database file. Every request goes through six checks before it touches any data, including who you are and what your role is allowed to see. The screens check for changes every few seconds, so a manager’s dashboard stays up to date without reloading. It’s built with Node.js, Express, SQLite and plain JavaScript.

Seeing every lead, every agent and every step in one place is a simple idea, but it’s the part of this project I’m proudest of.
