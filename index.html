CRM

In real estate a good lead rarely belongs to one agent, but most CRMs keep a single status per lead, so each update erases the last. In this CRM each sales agent keeps a private pipeline on the lead, and the admin sees all of them side by side.

  • Many agents, one lead. An 11-step feedback pipeline from New to Contract signed. Signing closes the lead for everyone.
  • Each role sees only what it needs. Sales can't see who brought the lead in or who else is working it.
  • Every promise on time. A diary, call-back reminders, and one tap to call or WhatsApp a client from the phone.
  • Full traceability. An audit trail nobody can edit, sales performance and win rates, and team leaders who run their teams.
  • Hardened. A security audit found and fixed 30 issues, including an account-takeover chain.

PROJECT MANAGER · 222 ENDPOINTS

Infinity

Infinity is a property marketplace in Arabic and English. Buying a home in Egypt usually means hopping between brokers, brochures and spreadsheets to compare compounds you have never seen. Infinity puts every unit, compound and price in one place, so buyers can find, compare and decide on their own. As project manager for a team of three developers, I delivered it on one API with 222 endpoints in five modules: public search, owners, signed-in users and a back office with role-based permissions.

1 · The map

Buyers choose location first, so the map is where the search starts. Zoomed out, compounds are grouped into one box per area so a whole city reads at a glance; zoom in and each compound appears with its real border and starting price, and switch to units to see every available home as its own dot. The result list follows whatever part of the map you are looking at, and a place search jumps straight to any neighbourhood. Why it matters: a buyer sees what is actually near their work, school or family before reading a single listing.

2 · Search and filters

Filter units and compounds by city, developer, type, price, area, delivery date and payment plan. Why it matters: budget and payment plan decide most purchases, so buyers only see homes they can actually afford, instead of scrolling past ones they can't.

3 · Side-by-side comparison

Compare two to four units or compounds in one table, with similar compounds suggested next to each one. Why it matters: the final decision is always between a few options, and putting them side by side replaces the notes and screenshots buyers would otherwise juggle.

4 · Client listings

Owners submit their own units for sale, and staff approve or reject each one before it goes live. Why it matters: it grows the inventory beyond the company's own projects while keeping every listing on the site checked and trustworthy.

5 · Tools for buyers

A mortgage calculator, an AI chatbot, favourites, saved searches with email alerts, and reviews. Why it matters: buyers can work out monthly payments, get answers at any hour and hear about a new match the day it is listed, which keeps them coming back until they buy.

6 · Secure by design

Password reset by one-time code that never reveals which emails exist, sign-out on every device, and role-based permissions for every back-office action. Why it matters: buyers share personal and financial details, and the marketplace only works if they trust it with them.

Lead Generator

A marketplace where real estate brokers buy fresh leads. Browsing is free: every lead shows the area, project, unit, budget and payment plan. Spend points to unlock the client's name, number and notes, and the lead becomes yours alone.

  • Browse free, pay per lead. Filter by area, project, unit type, budget and how fresh the lead is.
  • Points through billing. Buyers pay the billing team, staff add the points with a receipt reference, and the buyer is notified.
  • Every lead is sold once. Unlocking makes it yours and removes it for every other buyer, even if two people press Unlock at the same moment.
  • Wrong-number protection. Report a lead within 48 hours, and the points come back once it's confirmed.
  • Every point is traceable in a ledger of top-ups, unlocks, refunds and corrections.
  • Secure by design. Contact details never leave the server before purchase, and the app is tested against forged tokens, role escalation, SQL injection and XSS.

PERSONAL PROJECT

CyberVault

CyberVault is a local-first workspace for penetration testers. Every engagement teaches you something, and most of it ends up scattered across notes you never find again. CyberVault keeps it all in one place on your own machine, built around three main features:

  • The library: what you know, organised into domains and topics and shown as a knowledge map.
  • Methodologies: how you test, as reusable checklists you run on every target.
  • Case studies: the engagement you're working on right now, with a network map, an action log and a report.

1 · The library

A home for everything you learn. Add a domain for each area of study, break it into topics, and write your notes in any topic, with code, commands, diagrams and screenshots living together. Tag topics with hashtags, star the ones you keep coming back to, and the information you need is always a click away. The knowledge map turns the whole library into one visual mind map, so you see everything you know at a glance instead of digging through folders.

2 · Methodologies

A checklist for every kind of test. Build a methodology once, for a web app, a network, an AWS review or anything else you run, and reuse it on every engagement so nothing is skipped. Each checklist is fully yours to customise: add, remove and reorder items as your process grows. It is the difference between testing from memory and testing the same thorough way every time.

3 · Case studies

Where you run the engagement itself. Set the scenario, press Start, and every step you take is written to an action log as you go. The network map is your draft board for the target: drop in every host, service and finding, draw the attack paths between them, and pin notes anywhere on the board to think out loud. When you are done, CyberVault pulls the whole case, every action and every finding, into one clean report that makes the engagement clear at a glance, and saves it. That is what keeps all the cases you are working on organised instead of living in your head.

Why it matters

Together, the three features cover a pentester's whole workflow in one place: study it in the library, standardise it in a methodology, and run it as a case. Nothing you learn gets lost between engagements, no step gets skipped because you tested from memory, and every case ends in a clear report instead of living in your head. Your knowledge compounds instead of leaking away, so every new target starts from everything you have already learned.
